What’s Lost with a Look

Phase, Coherence and Entanglement Costs to Untouched Qubits on IBM Processors

Paper (PDF)

On this page, brackets are 95 % bootstrap intervals over the units each reading is taken over, qubits, Bell pairs, coupled pairs or qubit triples (2000 draws, 4000 in the Hahn runs), and readings combined over processors join them by value and width. ± is the spread of three jobs for the decay exponent and one shot-noise standard error for the Rigetti shifts.

  1. Each mid-circuit readout (with the default measure instruction) kicks the phase of qubits it never touches, compared with an idle of the same scheduled length in the same job. With 39 of 156 qubits read out, the median over the 117 untouched qubits is 1.73 [1.29, 2.21] (ibm_fez), 2.41 [1.96, 3.58] (ibm_kingston), 2.13 [1.44, 2.79] (ibm_marrakesh) and 1.79 [1.16, 2.66] (ibm_pittsburgh) degrees per readout in the 21 October run (kick analysis written after the data): 1.7 to 2.4 degrees on four IBM processors. An independent, timestamped control run gives 1.62, 2.86 and 2.39 degrees on fez, kingston and marrakesh (1.6 to 2.9 degrees), every interval clear of 0. The run’s planned ratio of readout to idle phase (1.17 to 1.20, plan hashed locally) depends on which slowly precessing qubits are kept and is not used. This is for the layout (every fourth qubit measured) in which every untouched qubit is within two couplings of a measured one. In the three reach layouts, where most untouched qubits are further away, the median over all untouched qubits is 0.06 to 0.13 degrees per readout (0.056 to 0.125). data · repeat
  2. On ibm_marrakesh the kick depends on which readout instruction is used (shorter instruction: 0.58 [0.43, 0.73] and 0.51 [0.36, 0.65] of the longer one’s kick at 4 and 8 readouts). On ibm_kingston the first run is not decided (at 8 readouts its two copies disagree). Two repeats followed. The first (1000 shots) reads 1.01 [0.81, 1.20] at 4 readouts, cannot tell because its copies disagree, and 1.01 [0.80, 1.37] at 8, per readout. The second (2000 shots) reads 0.95 [0.75, 1.17] and 0.98 [0.77, 1.28] at 4 and 8 readouts, consistent with the full kick and excluding the length ratio 0.63. Fez cannot tell, also at 4000 shots. readout-instruction run
  3. No wear-out is resolved over 72 readouts. Untouched qubits held in 0 while the measured qubits do 72 readouts or 72 idles, then started fresh on the equator (window coherence 0.53 to 0.76, so phases are readable), differ after 72 readouts against 72 equal idles (kick size after 72 readouts minus after 72 idles, positive when the kick is larger) by +0.028 [−0.038, +0.085] (ibm_fez, whose 72-idle arm had shifted copies), +0.006 [−0.034, +0.059] (ibm_kingston) and −0.036 [−0.118, +0.104] (ibm_marrakesh) degrees per readout, consistent with zero on all three. In the control and raw-readout runs, pooled, the kick after 8 readouts minus after 2, printed with the kick negative so that a positive value means a smaller kick at 8, is also consistent with zero (+0.036, −0.008, +0.294 degrees, at most 14, 6 and 24% of the kick). In a run of up to 81 readouts the later readouts read a smaller kick (0.63, 0.73, 0.43 degrees against 1.56, 2.82, 2.84 for readouts 1 to 9), but by 54 to 81 readouts 67 to 97 of 117 qubits are below five times the shot noise, so those readings cannot be interpreted. data
  4. The kick is not simply extra time: untouched qubits whose idle phase turns either way are kicked the same way. The 21 to 36 qubits per processor whose idle phase turns opposite to the majority are kicked by 1.0 to 3.4 degrees per readout (fez 1.18 and 0.96, kingston 3.03 and 2.80, marrakesh 3.37 and 1.52 in the control and raw-readout runs), every interval clear of 0. Extra time alone would kick the two groups in opposite directions. data
  5. It is not bigger when the measured qubits start in 1, each against an idle that also holds them in 1: in a timestamped run (22 October) the kick in 1 is 0.18 degrees (ibm_fez) and 0.92 degrees (ibm_marrakesh) per readout smaller than in 0. That run prints the kick as negative, so its difference (kick in 1 minus kick in 0) is positive when the kick in 1 is smaller. On ibm_kingston the first 22 October job’s kick in 0 had shifted copies (cannot tell) and the difference reads +0.066 [−0.005, +0.229], consistent with zero (no bound taken from it). A repeat job bounds any increase to about 0.1 degrees (+0.049 [−0.098, +0.132], copies agree). The 21 October excitation test, re-read after the data, prints the kick as positive, so there a positive difference means a larger kick in 1. Its difference is +0.204 [−0.092, +0.320] on kingston (larger in 1 by at most 0.32 degrees, consistent with zero) and −0.673 [−0.982, −0.403] on marrakesh (smaller in 1). The runs are reported beside each other and not combined. Holding the measured qubits in 1 without any readout shifts the untouched qubits’ phase by 2.5 to 3.4 degrees per step (22 October, and 3.1 degrees on 21 October), which is why the comparison needs the matched idle. data · data · kingston repeat
  6. A cause that does not depend on the number of qubits read out is excluded: on the same schedule, reading out 1 of the 39 qubits leaves at most 0.15 degrees on ibm_fez and 0.09 degrees on ibm_kingston (95 % bounds), and at most 0.15 degrees on ibm_marrakesh by a copy-gap check defined after the data (the committed rule reads its two copies as disagreeing). Reading out all 39 gives the full kick, and a fixed offset of the full kick is excluded on all three (every copy at least 1.4, 2.7 and 2.2 degrees from it). Reading out 10 of the 39 gives 0.13, 0.28 and 0.23 degrees (8 to 10% of the full kick, less than the proportional 26%), so the kick is not proportional to the number read out. On fez the copies of that reading disagree by the committed rule, with no shift by copy gap (pooled 0.13, and a 22 October repeat with the same code reads 0.17 [0.07, 0.32] degrees with both copies agreeing, 11% of that job’s full kick of 1.47). The marrakesh copies read 0.32 and 0.48, and the pooled median is smaller than either. The subsets of 1 and of 10 are one random draw (seed 20261021), and the single qubit is one position. The kick size next to a measured qubit minus the size two steps away is −0.612, −1.599 and +0.332 degrees (fez, kingston, marrakesh), resolved on none. data
  7. It reaches untouched qubits that are not wired to any measured qubit. With the 39 packed in one region, untouched qubits 3 to 4 couplings from the nearest one are kicked by 0.81, 1.77 and 1.30 degrees per readout (fez, kingston, marrakesh, over 15 qubits, every interval above 0). Within two couplings in that layout only kingston is resolved (3.458 degrees, over 10 qubits), and at five or more couplings (92 qubits) the kick is consistent with zero on all three (+0.0039 [−0.029, +0.037] combined). That pool is dominated by qubits six or more couplings away, and at exactly 5 the reach run reads +0.055 [+0.002, +0.108] combined, at the false-reading floor. A second run built to find where it stops (three packed layouts) resolves it at 4 couplings with the three processors combined (size +0.21 [+0.09, +0.32] degrees per readout, where the kick itself is negative, about a tenth of the kick next to a measured qubit) and at 3 couplings on ibm_fez and ibm_marrakesh singly, while 5 couplings is resolved combined (+0.055 [+0.002, +0.108]) and by no processor alone. From 6 couplings on, the kick is consistent with zero combined (upper ends 0.03 to 0.06 degrees), and ibm_marrakesh alone reads 6 couplings above zero, +0.050 [+0.002, +0.155]. The size depends on the layout: per-layout medians at the same distance differ several-fold. Readings were not corrected for multiplicity. Synthetic runs with no kick resolve 6.0% of single-processor readings and 5.6% of combined ones, so the resolutions at 3 couplings on fez, at 5 couplings combined and at 6 couplings on marrakesh, whose lower ends are 0.001 to 0.002, are not distinguished from that floor. reach · reach run
  8. Readouts cost untouched qubits superposition, the coherence that T2 describes: the median untouched qubit next to a measured one, keeping only shots in which every measured neighbour was verified in its 0 cluster on every readout (97 to 99% of shots), keeps 0.0056 and 0.0062 less coherence than after the same idle on ibm_kingston and 0.0087 and 0.0205 less on ibm_marrakesh after 4 and 8 readouts: 0.006 to 0.021, all above zero when the copies are pooled. On kingston at 4 readouts neither copy alone resolves it. At 2 readouts the committed rule reads kingston and marrakesh as cannot tell, because their two copies disagree, and a copy-gap check defined after the data finds no shift, with pooled readings above zero, 0.0054 and 0.0074. On ibm_fez the committed rule reads 2 and 4 readouts as cannot tell for the same reason. The same copy-gap check finds no shift between its copies, and the pooled reading, above zero, is 0.004 to 0.006. At 8 readouts it is consistent with zero. In the control run the cost after 8 readouts minus after 2 is +0.0043 on fez and +0.0120 on marrakesh (above zero) and +0.0047 on kingston (not resolved), and the 31 untouched qubits per processor with no measured neighbour show no resolved coherence loss there. In the 21 October superposition run the readout-minus-idle coherence at 8 readouts is −0.011 (fez), −0.005 (kingston) and −0.013 (marrakesh), intervals below 0, and at 32 readouts −0.010, −0.006 and −0.014 with intervals reaching 0. Its planned loss ratio at 32 readouts is consistent with 1 on all three. Whether the cost compounds past 8 readouts cannot be read with these qubits (at 32 readouts the intervals reach 0). The first X-flip check is void (its known-good condition was not met on any processor). In the second (flip check) a neighbour hidden in a level that reads as 0 is excluded as the cause on ibm_marrakesh, to the extent the X-flip catches it. On fez the check cannot tell, and on kingston its known-good condition was not met. Neighbours stuck in 1 (0.4 to 1.1 % of shots) are not excluded as the whole cost. data · controls · raw-readout run
  9. Readouts cost untouched Bell pairs entanglement: 0.005 to 0.009 less concurrence than the same idle after 4 readouts, in shots where every measured neighbour was verified in 0 (pairs whose idle concurrence is above 0). This selection can bias the loss upward, and the 39-minus-1 difference, which applies it to both arms, is the unbiased reading. Resolved on ibm_fez (+0.0051 [+0.0005, +0.0126], first run +0.0070) and ibm_marrakesh (+0.0094 [+0.0037, +0.0243], first run +0.0093) in two independent runs each, read side by side and never pooled, and on marrakesh also with the pairs wired to three poorly read measured qubits removed. On ibm_kingston the committed rule reads the clean-shot reading as cannot tell in either run (the copies disagree). A copy-gap check defined after the data finds no shift in either, and the pooled readings are above zero, +0.0100 [+0.0040, +0.0138] and +0.0068 [+0.0010, +0.0170]. The all-shot reading is above zero in both. The loss with 39 qubits read out exceeds the loss with 1 read out by 0.0088, 0.0104 and 0.0065 (marrakesh, kingston, fez) in the first run, where fez is a cannot-tell by the committed rule, with no shift by a copy-gap check defined after the data (+0.0065 [+0.0032, +0.0107]). In the repeat it is above zero on kingston and marrakesh and consistent with zero on fez. So a time shift per step that is the same whatever number of qubits is read is excluded on kingston and marrakesh by the committed rule in both runs, and on fez only in the first run, by a copy-gap reading defined after the data. The control run’s pre-registered reading over all shots is a loss on all three processors (kingston 0.011 and 0.010 at 2 and 4 readouts, marrakesh 0.009 and 0.016, fez 0.005 at 4). In that run’s selection (every measured neighbour read 0 on every readout) the marrakesh cost is consistent with zero at 2 and 4 readouts. A Bell-pair run on marrakesh (plan hashed locally) lost almost all concurrence by 8 steps in both arms and cannot tell. data · repeat
  10. A qubit that is read out loses more than idling does. At equal elapsed time, with no assumption about the shape of the decay curve, this comparison is from ibm_fez (11 October) and the 22 October readout-instruction run (fez and marrakesh, and kingston in a repeat). On fez, 24 readouts leave 0.48 to 0.52 of the signal where 40 µs of idle in the same jobs leaves 0.62 to 0.66. On all seven processors the loss also follows under an approximate one-exponential conversion, which these qubits do not follow exactly. With it, a measured qubit (per-processor mean of three reads, 4 to 6 October, each read over about 30 qubits, single reads range 1.2 to 1.8) loses per readout what idling 1.5 to 1.7 stated readout lengths would cost it on ibm_fez, ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh, and 1.3 to 1.4 on four more IBM processors. In the 21 October control run (bound defined after the data) the 95 % upper bounds on how much longer than stated the readout actually lasts are 4, 1 and 12 % on the first three (rounded up from 3.7, 0.8 and 11.4 %). A readout 50 to 70 % longer than stated (30 to 40 % on the other four) would be needed to explain this loss as a longer readout. Those bounds exclude extra time as the cause of the coherence cost on kingston (2 and 4 readouts) and do not exclude it on fez and marrakesh. The comparison of 39 read out with 1 read out excludes it on fez and marrakesh and cannot tell on kingston. data · readout length

Controls and a second vendor

  1. Idle qubits do not decay along a simple exponential: on six IBM processors (26 jobs, 4 to 7 October), 62 to 85 % of qubit-copies prepared in 1 read below the line from their own 300 µs level at 30 µs (levels are raw readout signal normalised with the job’s own off and on references), where a pure exponential passed through the same normalisation, with the same shots and noise, gives 49 to 54 % (re-check, 22 Oct). The test cannot separate a non-exponential decay from a per-qubit systematic common to the three copies. data
  2. A qubit prepared in 0 and read after a 300 µs idle sits 0.04 to 0.80 % above its own job’s zero-wait 0 level in all 26 jobs on six processors (five of them under two widths, the smallest, +0.04%, at 0.3 widths), while at 600 µs three jobs are at or below zero, so the offset does not grow but is not constant. data
  3. Repeated measurements of a qubit’s decay time (median over the chip, three copies per job) give 168 to 183 µs on marrakesh, 260 to 270 µs on kingston (4 October, three jobs each) and 294 to 297 µs in the first of three pittsburgh jobs, and differ by up to 9.8 µs between jobs and 11.5 µs between copies inside one job. Compared like with like, the range of three copies in a job is inside the simulated 95th percentile of shot noise alone in 8 of 9 jobs (marrakesh job 301: 10.4 against 8.9 µs). The simulation takes its noise scale from the copies themselves, so it cannot separate shot noise from drift between copies. data
  4. One coupled neighbour in superposition makes an untouched qubit’s coherence beat at a scale s of IBM’s calibrated static ZZ for that pair: 0.916 [0.869, 0.983] and 0.981 [0.938, 1.005] on ibm_fez, 0.514 [0.456, 0.673] and 0.420 [0.303, 0.558] on ibm_phoenix (22 October). On fez’s own geometry the fit reads 0.875 to 0.908 when the scale is 1, so the fez values are consistent with 1. A run on 37 further phoenix pairs, with fit and analysis fixed before the job, finds the beat does not scale with the calibrated value: log-log slope 0.19 [0.01, 0.38], against 1.00 [0.89, 1.14] for the same fit on fez, with beats of 4 to 23 kHz for calibrated values of 11.6 to 58.8 kHz (37 fitted pairs). A second run on the same pairs (23 October), also fixed before the job, reads the shift directly with the neighbour in 0 and then in 1: log-log slope −0.06 [−0.63, 0.32], or 0.19 [0.04, 0.37] without two edges whose fits slipped, and a median of 0.49 [0.45, 0.53] of the calibrated value, while the same code on fez reads 0.92 [0.83, 1.02] and 1.04. Three more phoenix edges, and two on ibm_fez, are calibrated as 0.00 in the record in force although IBM’s earlier records gave them 2.9 to 17.5 kHz, and they shift by 2.85 to 15.4 kHz (record history). A single convention factor would leave the slope near 1 (an outside referee’s simulation gives 1.05 for a factor of two). This rests on one processor and two runs, and the cause is not identified. data · flat-beat run · direct-shift run
  5. IBM states T2 from a Hahn echo. Our Hahn echo on the same qubits, thirteen runs on six processors (22 and 23 October), reads delivered over stated T2, median over each chip’s qubits, under the planned fit and under a fit with a free asymptote (defined after the data): kingston 0.910 to 0.957 and 0.916 to 0.984 (three runs), marrakesh 0.907 to 1.015 and 0.892 to 0.958 (three runs), fez 1.000 to 1.051 and 0.977 to 1.006 (four runs), pittsburgh 1.146 and 1.040, phoenix 0.911 and 0.989, boston 1.019 and 0.899. The stated T2 values were 0.5 to 34.3 hours old when the runs ran, and under the planned fit the reading moves between jobs under one unchanged record. data
  6. On Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q, a neighbour in |2⟩ shifts a coupled qubit’s precession rate by −54.93 ± 1.28 degrees per µs on 28 September and −38.64 ± 1.62 degrees per µs on 4 October (shot noise only), and the dates differ by 16.3 ± 2.1 degrees per µs. Between the two dates the pulse amplitude was retuned, the number of simultaneous qubits changed from 1 to 106 and the processor was recalibrated, so the difference is not assigned to the coupling. Over 22 other pairs the shift spans −72.8 to +30.9 degrees per µs: on this processor sign and size are properties of the pair. There is no readout-against-idle design on this processor. data

Earlier runs, echo and gates

  1. Equal-idle runs of 7 October, with 5 to 25 measured qubits, resolve a kick of the same sign within two couplings (0.3 to 3.1 degrees per readout). These runs print the kick as negative, as the 22 October runs do. On kingston the 14 arms with 25 measured qubits (six from the same three jobs, on overlapping untouched qubits) read the kick over all untouched qubits below zero in 13 of them (0.26 to 0.58 degrees per readout), with the measured qubits in 0, in 1 or on the equator, back to back or with 3 µs gaps. Within two couplings it is below zero in 12 of 14 arms (0.69 to 1.38 degrees). At five or more couplings no arm is below zero, and one arm reads above zero (+0.175 [+0.036, +0.311], reversed order), not distinguished from the false-reading floor. A corner layout with 12 measured qubits, where most untouched qubits are five or more couplings away, is consistent with zero overall. These runs differ from those of 21 and 22 October in the number of measured qubits, the neighbour held in 1, the number of readouts and the copy test, and are read beside them, never pooled. They are recomputed with the paper’s estimators and are not pre-registered. data
  2. A symmetric echo (X, X) on the untouched qubits in every step of both arms removes most of the kick: per readout −1.50, −3.24 and −2.50 degrees without the echo and −0.09, +0.45 and −0.27 degrees with it (fez, kingston, marrakesh). This run prints the kick as negative, while the first item above gives its size. With the echo the residual is resolved on all three, including +0.45 on kingston, opposite in sign, and is not combined. These runs do not separate a shift during the readout from a control-system phase-frame update. Whether the echo changes the coherence cost cannot be told. data
  3. Spaced X-gate trains: at intervals of 30 µs and longer, a decay curve that is faster than one exponential right after preparation removes most of the few-percent excess after spaced X-gate trains under one-exponential bookkeeping, and over-corrects by 1 to 2% on marrakesh. At 5 and 10 µs a residual remains, and these runs cannot tell whether it is physical. The same decay shape reproduces a negative per-gate residual (−0.0006 to −0.0020 per gate on four processors). With the decay shape held equal, an extra X gate costs 0.0001643 [0.00005906, 0.0002695] combined over fez, kingston, marrakesh and phoenix. On fez and kingston the per-gate residual is opposite in sign to the extra-gate cost and at least twice its upper edge, so it is not a gate cost there. No extra-gate test exists on boston or pittsburgh. This is not a claim that the gates are better than stated. data · extra-gate test

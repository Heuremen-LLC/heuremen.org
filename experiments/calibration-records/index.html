IBM calibration records, September 2026

IBM’s own calibration records as we pulled them, behind the record notes of the Quantum Idle Audit QIA-2026-001 (the audit, PDF). The notes are numbered as in that PDF. The October records the other notes use are saved beside each run, in that run’s folder.

  • Record notes 4 and 5 (T1 and T2 dates, kingston and marrakesh, records stamped 26 to 28 September): ibm_kingston, ibm_marrakesh, and ibm_fez records of the same days: ibm_fez. One gzipped JSON per day, as returned by IBM.
  • Record note 6 (T1 and T2 under a new record stamp): the daily medians of six processors, 15 September to 6 October, flagged 8 day pairs (a ninth, phoenix 6 Oct 12:25, was pulled after) whose record stamp changed while the T1/T2 medians did not. The per-qubit check pulls both records of each pair from IBM’s properties history (no job) and compares every qubit: all 2,655 T1 and T2 entries of 16 September to 6 October (9 record pairs, including a third phoenix record of 6 October) keep the same value and the same measurement date under the new stamp (script, output). Five more record pairs of 22 and 23 October, on fez, kingston and marrakesh, hold 1,549 entries, all identical in value and date (script, output). In all, 4,204 of 4,204 entries are identical across 14 record pairs, 16 September to 23 October, the figure the audit prints.
  • Record note 7 (zz on five live edges): zz_history.py reads every published record (output): fez 13–14 and 39–53 at 7.45 and 2.89 kHz on 26 to 29 September, phoenix 100–101, 70–80, 86–96 at 17.46, 14.13 and 13.68 kHz on 5 and 6 October, all 0.00 in the records of 21 and 22 October.
  • Record notes 3 to 5, 8 and 9: record_notes.py (output). The script’s own header still numbers them 2 to 4, 6 and 7, from an earlier version of the audit.
  • A same-hour read of every qubit’s readout error against IBM’s stated readout fields, 22 October: readout audit.

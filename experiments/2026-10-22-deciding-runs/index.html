  • Does the superposition cost compound per readout? Cannot tell: the processors disagree at 9 and at 27 readouts, and past 27 most untouched qubits are at the noise floor. Per readout, as a fraction of the coherence left: 0.12, 0.17 and 0.53 % at 9 readouts and 0.13, 0.25 and 0.39 % at 27 (fez, kingston, marrakesh); the processors disagree (chip-to-chip χ²/dof 7.17 and 2.86), so no combined value is quoted. At 27 to 81 readouts the coherence of most untouched qubits is at the noise floor, where its magnitude is biased upward in both arms and the ratio is pulled toward 1; the per-readout share at 81 and the sum-form comparison are not read.
  • Cost of an extra X gate. With the decay shape held equal (X X X against X on the same waits), an extra X gate costs 0.0001643 [0.00005906, 0.0002695] combined over four processors (fez, kingston, marrakesh, phoenix), above 0. Against IBM’s stated X error the difference is −0.0002279 [−0.0003419, −0.0001139] combined; on marrakesh alone it is below the stated value, on the other three consistent with it. This train does not see everything the stated error includes, so “below” is not a claim that the gates are better than stated.   • The kick is not bigger with the measured qubits in 1 (claim 4; plan pushed 02:37 UTC before the jobs, excitation.py, analyze_excitation.py, output). Kick in 1 minus kick in 0, per step: +0.178 fez, +0.06599 kingston, +0.9226° marrakesh [kingston: its kick in 0 had shifted copies (cannot tell), and the difference, −0.005397 to +0.229, is consistent with zero; no bound taken from it].   • GHZ flatness. Not run in these deciding runs: P(000) + P(111) with τ/3 offsets is exactly constant (1/16) in textbook quantum mechanics, so its flatness ratio is infinite in theory; a ratio measured in an earlier run would show how close the hardware comes to an identity, not a physical effect.
  • Coherence comes back on ibm_phoenix. On ibm_phoenix (square lattice), untouched qubits’ coherence falls to the measurement floor by about 26 µs when their neighbours are held in superposition and then returns briefly at 58–78 µs (+0.039 [+0.020, +0.046] above the 26–50 µs level, n = 60; 48 of 60 qubits); on ibm_fez the same quantity keeps falling (−0.168 [−0.204, −0.086], n = 92); neighbour on the equator minus neighbour in 0 is +0.243 [+0.211, +0.263] on phoenix and +0.002 [−0.017, +0.025] on fez. On phoenix, with neighbours in 0 the coherence only decays (−0.204 [−0.221, −0.187]), and with neighbours in 1 the copies disagree (cannot tell). ibm_fez shows no come-back within 150 µs in any state (−0.1725, −0.2389, −0.1678). An outside referee’s model after the data, using IBM’s calibrated per-edge ZZ scaled by a fitted factor of about 0.5 on phoenix, tracks the curves better than shuffled couplings do (0 of 60 shuffles); whether phoenix’s calibrated value is stated under a different convention is not known from these data; the pre-registered flat-beat run later found the beat does not scale with the calibrated value (slope 0.19 against 1), which a single convention factor would not produce. The processors disagree far beyond their widths and are not combined. Outside referee’s recomputation and model: load.py, model.py, model2.py. A free fit by the referee after the data (not pre-registered, not in the paper) puts the beat near 12.5 kHz, which would return near 80 µs, where this come-back sat; see the one-neighbour run.

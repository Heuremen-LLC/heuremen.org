Controls: decay, idle, drift

Runs. Decay-design and idle-control jobs on IBM processors, 1–7 October 2026 (job IDs in the data files).

On this page, ± is the analytic shot-noise standard error of the mean of three reads, including the shot error of each qubit’s own 300 µs reference.

What holds

  • Idle-control residuals against each qubit’s own decay (ibm_pittsburgh): +0.0017 ± 0.0013, +0.0020 ± 0.0014, +0.0014 ± 0.0014.
  • A qubit left in 0 for 300 µs sits 0.04 to 0.80 % above its own job’s zero-wait level in all 26 decay-design jobs on six processors (five of them under two widths, the smallest, +0.04 %, at 0.3 widths). At 600 µs three jobs are at or below zero, so the offset does not grow but is not constant.
  • Decay-time copies within jobs: marrakesh 174.0 / 177.3 / 180.7 µs, kingston 260.4–265.6, pittsburgh 293.8–297.1. The largest job-to-job move of any copy is 9.8 µs, and copies within one marrakesh job span 10.4 µs.
  • Measured-qubit loss over untouched-qubit time per chip-day: 1.207–1.784.

Run it

Download the script and the files in data/ into one folder (data in data/), then python3 verify_processors_apparatus.py. Standard library only. It prints each figure beside the recomputed value. The compact data were cut from the raw results. The raw files and the reduction scripts are available on request.

Outside referee check, 22 October, after the data (referee_controls.py, output): the 300 µs idle-in-0 offset is positive in all 26 jobs, does not grow by 600 µs and is not constant (mean 600 minus 300: −0.11 %, higher in 9 of 26, and three jobs at or below zero at 600 µs). Compared like with like, the observed range of three copies (3.3 to 11.5 µs) is inside the simulated 95th percentile of the range of three in 8 of 9 jobs (marrakesh job 301: 10.4 against 8.9 µs). The simulation takes its noise scale from the copies, so it cannot separate shot noise from drift. Decay times differ by up to 9.8 µs between jobs and 11.5 µs between copies inside one job. Null check for the non-exponential share (observed 61.5–84.8 %, 26 jobs, from a separate computation in the 22 October re-check file). The paper’s figures, recomputed by verify_processors_apparatus.py on this page, are 62.4 to 85.3 %.

Decay of a measured qubit under repeated readout

Run. ibm_fez, 11 October 2026 (IBM job IDs in the data file). A qubit prepared in 1 is read out 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 or 128 times. Untouched qubits nearby run a Ramsey sequence, and an idle arm of 40 µs sits in each job.

This page rests on three jobs. ibm_fez ran 14 jobs that day, and the other 11 are a three-box probe-strength sweep.

What holds

On this page, ± is the sample standard deviation of the fitted exponent across the three jobs.

  • The measured qubit’s population after k readouts follows a three-parameter stretched exponential in every job (χ²/dof 0.72 / 1.38 / 1.32), exponent 0.823 ± 0.012 (spread of three jobs).
  • The untouched qubits’ Ramsey coherence follows a stretched exponential with exponent 1.381 ± 0.074 (a different quantity, so the two exponents are not compared as one effect).
  • Population 0.965 after one readout and 0.067 after 128 (job 1). It crosses 1/2 after 25.5, 22.6 and 23.9 readouts in the three jobs.
  • At equal elapsed time, 24 readouts (39.8 µs) leave 0.517 / 0.481 / 0.498, and 40 µs of idle leaves 0.661 / 0.623 / 0.647.

The script also prints a stored “measured” length of one ibm_kingston readout (2.323 µs on 4 October, 2.402 on 6 October, against 2.180 stated). That figure comes from the earlier fitted-rate method, which converts the untouched qubits’ extra phase into time and so includes the readout kick (October campaign). It does not measure how long the readout lasts. The reading that tests duration is the extra-time bound of the 21 October control run, at most 1 % on kingston.

Run it

Download the script and the files in data/ into one folder (data in data/), then python3 verify_cost.py. Standard library only. It prints each figure beside the recomputed value. The compact data were cut from the raw results. The raw files and the reduction scripts are available on request.

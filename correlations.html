Temporal and spatial correlations, and a three-box weak measurement

IBM processors, one evening. 24 September 2026.

Correlation across time matches correlation across space, to the same cosine, on the same chip. A qubit entangled with a partner reads nearly blank in its own frame, length about 0.05. And in a three-box weak measurement the third box has a negative weak value, 8.9 to 12 error bars below zero on three chips. Three chips, one evening. All three are what standard quantum mechanics predicts; the point of this page is that they were measured cheaply, and every number is in the linked files.

The question that motivated the runs: do correlations across time (one qubit measured twice) and across space (two entangled qubits) follow the same law on real hardware? We built small experiments on IBM quantum processors and asked the chips.

Every number on this page is drawn straight from the result files, which are linked at the bottom so you can rerun all of it yourself.

The temporal and spatial correlators follow the same cos θ.

2 · The arrow

A rotation can be undone; a measurement cannot.

A qubit in full superposition has coherence near 1. Twist it and untwist it: it comes right back. Look at it once: it’s gone, and nothing undoes a look. Meanwhile the correlation between two measurements is the same whether the rotation runs forward or backward. This is the textbook asymmetry between unitary evolution and measurement, seen directly on the chip.

coherence: start 0.98 → twist and untwist 0.98 → after one look +0.02

ibm_marrakesh, qubits 1, 2, 133. Replicated on ibm_kingston and ibm_fez (−0.925 / −0.919 at π).

3 · One qubit of an entangled pair, read alone

A qubit entangled with a partner has no state of its own.

A qubit alone in superposition carries its own state: a point with length 0.99 inside the sphere of possibilities. Entangle it and look at it by itself — all three directions read about zero. Length 0.06 and 0.05 for the two pairs. Nearly blank in its own frame. The information didn’t vanish; it moved into the link with its partner.

length of the qubit’s own state (1 = fully its own, 0 = blank). ibm_marrakesh; replicated on ibm_kingston (pair 144-145, 0.991 → 0.061) and ibm_fez (0.995 → 0.064).

4 · The silent corner

A negative weak value, as the three-box paradox predicts.

The three-box paradox (Aharonov & Vaidman, 1991). Spread one particle across boxes A, B, C. Much later, keep only the runs that end in one particular arrangement. In between, peek with a probe so gentle it barely touches anything — an outside look that doesn’t know what it’s looking for. Sorted by what happened later, the gentle probe reports: A holds 1, B holds 1, and C holds −1. Two qubits have four slots; the fourth corner, 11, is never used, and it stays silent.

A second encoding. Two qubits have four basis states; we placed the box that should read negative on 11, the state an ordinary measurement of the prepared and post-selected states never returns. The third qubit, the weak probe, read it. The corner 11 read −0.51 ± 0.06 — the ideal value is −1, and hardware noise shrinks weak values toward zero; this is nine standard errors below zero — while boxes 00 and 01 read +0.77 and +0.76, and the three together summed to 1.03: one particle. (ibm_marrakesh; the unused slot 10 leaked +0.17 ± 0.06, noted, not hidden.)

The prediction record

Twice a chip handed us a clean-looking ~1% effect. Once it was the order the circuits ran in; once an unbalanced partner qubit. Balanced reruns killed both, and both are in the table because a prediction record with the misses taken out is not a record.

This is established physics, measured cheaply and said plainly.

How this squares with the October runs

This page found that entanglement itself costs nothing extra: an entangled pair loses the same coherence as a matched unentangled control (+0.0012 ± 0.0024 at zero wait), and the two runs that first looked like a ~1 % extra were the circuit order and an unbalanced partner, removed by balanced reruns.

The October runs ask a different question: what does measuring other qubits do to qubits nobody touched? On 21 October, untouched qubits gained a phase kick of 1.7 to 2.4° per readout on four processors, and after 8 readouts kept 0.005–0.013 less coherence than after an equal idle on three.
